Title

AI After ChatGPT: A 2022–2026 Timeline for Insights Teams

Introduction

Since ChatGPT opened to the public on November 30, 2022, AI tools have changed every few months: new models, image and voice input, video generation, memory, connections to company data, and the first broad AI law. Research and brand teams have to decide which of these changes actually affect their work. This artifact takes twelve developments from Beyond the Chatbox, the interactive timeline my group built for the 1.3 discussion, and explains each one for insights and brand teams.

Description

The artifact is a timeline of twelve developments from November 2022 to October 2026, grouped into four periods. Each entry keeps the group’s label (major milestone or minor change) and gives three things: what happened, why it matters for research work, and the original source. A one-page PDF version is available for meetings or onboarding. The full group project, with all 25 events, a guided presentation and an audience quiz, is linked below.

Download the one-page PDF Open the full group site: Beyond the Chatbox

Group 10 (1.3 discussion): Irfan Siddiqui, Ali Bakhareba, Vikram NGN and Tanvir Kapadiya. The research-team selection and notes on this page are my own adaptation of our group work.

The timeline

The starting point · 2022
Nov 30, 2022

ChatGPT opens to the public

Starting point

OpenAI released ChatGPT as a free research preview, putting a conversational interface around GPT-3.5.

Why it matters for research: Analysts, clients and respondents got the same tool on the same day. Expectations for how fast insights can be turned around changed quickly, and some open-ended survey answers may now be written with AI help, which adds a new data-quality check.

Source: OpenAI (2022)
Wider access and competition · 2023
Mar 14, 2023

GPT-4 arrives

Major milestone

A more capable model that can also take images as input, with access expanding in stages.

Why it matters for research: Image input means a model can read a chart, a shelf photo or a piece of packaging. That helps desk research, but every reading still has to be checked against the source.

Source: OpenAI (2023a)
Jul 18, 2023

Llama 2 expands access to model weights

Major milestone

Meta released pretrained and chat model weights under a license that permits many commercial uses.

Why it matters for research: With downloadable weights, a team can run a model on its own systems. That matters when respondent or client data is not allowed to leave the company.

Source: Meta (2023)
Jul 20, 2023

Custom instructions save repetition

Minor / incremental

Users can save guidance that ChatGPT applies to future conversations.

Why it matters for research: A team can save its coding frame, tone or reporting rules once instead of retyping them, which makes AI-assisted work more consistent across analysts.

Source: OpenAI (2023b)
Media, reasoning and rules · 2024
Feb 13, 2024

Memory testing begins

Minor / incremental

Selected users began testing a ChatGPT feature that remembers useful information across conversations, with controls to manage it.

Why it matters for research: Convenient, but it raises a data-handling question: what client or respondent details might the tool keep? Teams need a rule before anyone pastes study data in.

Source: OpenAI (2024a)
Feb 15, 2024

Sora demonstrates text-to-video

Major milestone

OpenAI showed a research preview of generating high-fidelity video from text. It was not a general public launch.

Why it matters for research: Video concepts for testing may one day be produced much faster. The preview-versus-launch difference matters: research teams often hear about a tool long before they can use it.

Source: OpenAI (2024b)
May 13, 2024

GPT-4o brings text, vision and audio together

Major milestone

One model works across text, images and audio, with access released in stages.

Why it matters for research: Real-time voice opens the door to AI-moderated interviews. The same caution applies as with any moderator: check what the system actually captured.

Source: OpenAI (2024c)
Aug 1, 2024

The EU AI Act enters into force

Major milestone

The European Union’s broad legal framework for AI entered into force and began a phased implementation.

Why it matters for research: Teams working with EU customers or data need to know which risk category their AI use falls into and what transparency it requires.

Source: European Commission (2024)
Sep 12, 2024

o1-preview emphasizes reasoning

Major milestone

A new model series spends more computation working through a problem before answering.

Why it matters for research: Helpful for multi-step analysis questions, such as checking the logic of a segmentation, but each step still needs a human check.

Source: OpenAI (2024d)
Nov 25, 2024

The Model Context Protocol is introduced

Major milestone

Anthropic introduced an open protocol for connecting AI assistants to tools and data sources.

Why it matters for research: Assistants can now be connected to the places research data lives, such as files, databases and dashboards. That saves copying and pasting, but it makes access permissions a research-governance issue.

Source: Anthropic (2024)
Reasoning, tasks and privacy · 2025–2026
Aug 7, 2025

GPT-5 combines response styles

Major milestone

OpenAI introduced a system that combines fast responses with deeper reasoning.

Why it matters for research: The tool now decides how hard to work on a question. Analysts should still say when they need a careful answer, and they should still verify the numbers.

Source: OpenAI (2025)
Oct 20, 2026

Locked chats begin rolling out

Minor / incremental

Selected ChatGPT conversations can be hidden behind an account PIN or supported device authentication.

Why it matters for research: A small change that shows privacy is now a product feature. It limits who can open a chat on a shared device; it does not replace a team’s own rules on what data goes into AI tools.

Source: OpenAI (2026)

Objective

In the 1.3 discussion, our class talked through the major and minor AI developments since ChatGPT launched, and my group turned that discussion into Beyond the Chatbox. That site was written for our classmates. For this portfolio I had two goals. The first was my own: to understand which of these changes matter most in the field I work in. The second was for my audience. Insights managers, brand managers and research agency teams are asked to adopt new AI tools quickly, and they need a short way to see which changes touch study design, data handling and quality checks.

Process

  1. Started from our group’s 1.3 work. I used the 25-event timeline in Beyond the Chatbox, which I built with Irfan Siddiqui, Vikram NGN and Tanvir Kapadiya.
  2. Picked an audience and a question. I asked of every event: does it change how a research team designs studies, handles data or checks findings? Twelve events passed.
  3. Grouped the twelve into four periods so a reader can follow the story from the first public launch to recent privacy changes.
  4. Kept the group’s distinctions. I kept the major and minor labels and the difference between previews, staged rollouts and public launches, and checked each date against the original source the group cited.
  5. Wrote a “why it matters for research” note for each event, drawing on my work in brand tracking, survey design and retail measurement.
  6. Built the web version and a one-page PDF, then reviewed both against the course artifact template and the rubric.

Tools and Technologies Used

  • Claude: help with drafting, layout and the HTML/CSS code for this portfolio version. I selected the twelve events, wrote the research notes and checked the dates and references myself.
  • Primary sources from OpenAI, Google, Meta, Anthropic and the European Commission: to confirm dates and descriptions

Value Proposition

This timeline gives insights and brand teams a five-minute way to see which AI changes since ChatGPT actually affect their studies, from data privacy to voice and video, so they can ask sharper questions before adopting a new tool.

Unique Value

Most AI timelines, including our group site, are written for a general audience. This version reads the same events from inside market research. It puts weight on questions that matter to research teams but rarely appear in product announcements: where respondent data goes, whether a tool is really available or only previewed, and what still needs a human check. That angle comes from more than twelve years of running brand health, retail and survey studies.

Relevance

The artifact supports my personal value proposition: bringing AI into research carefully, with each claim checked and explained in plain language. It is relevant now because research teams are adopting generative AI faster than they are setting rules for it, and the EU AI Act has made AI use a compliance question as well as a quality question. For readers, the timeline is a starting point for judging new tools. For me, it is a base for the rest of this course, where I expect to work hands-on with machine learning methods.
